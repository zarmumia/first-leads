# Facebook groups guide

## Scanning
- Open each group from `channels.md` sorted by new posts (Sort → New posts).
- Read posts from roughly the last 24 hours. Older posts are rarely worth a reply.
- Open "See more" to read the full post before scoring; the real question is often at the end, and authors sometimes update the post.
- Read the existing comments. If someone already gave the advice you would give, either build on it ("Adding to what X said…") or skip.
- Anonymous posts are fine to answer in the thread; you cannot DM them anyway.

## Good candidates
- A member asks for help, a recommendation, or ideas about a problem in the user's field.
- A member describes the pain without naming it.
- Few comments so far, or comments that miss the real issue.

## Skip
- Ads, job posts, self-promotion, polls with no real question.
- Threads that turned into arguments.
- Groups whose rules forbid this kind of reply.

## Drafting
- Reply in the thread, in the language of the post.
- **The comment is about them, not about the user.** No "I build", "my clients", "at my business", no business name. People smell marketing from a mile away; the marketing is the quality of the comment, and anyone curious will open the profile (which is why the get-ready stage matters).
- Give one real, usable piece of value in the comment itself: an insight, a practical step, or a good question.
- 2–5 lines, written like a person in the group, not like a company.
- End with an open door (a concrete offer or a question), never "DM me".
- No links unless the group allows them and the person asked; at most one link per session.
- Vary the wording: never reuse the same opening twice in a session.
- Never open members' profiles or collect personal details.

## Supplier requests: comment + private message

When someone asks for a supplier ("I need a video editor for…", "looking for someone who can…", "send portfolios"), this is the strongest kind of lead. Treat it differently from a help question:
- **Priority:** show it first, with the label `SUPPLIER REQUEST`. Speed matters most here; these posts fill up within hours.
- **Check:** the post or the group rules do not say "comments only" or forbid private messages. If they do, comment only.
- **Draft two things:**
  1. A short comment in the thread: "Sent you a private message 🙂" or one line that answers their main requirement. Visible, not salesy.
  2. A private message, tailored to the request:
     - One line showing you read their request (their project, their deadline, their style).
     - The one or two most relevant samples from the profile's proof, not everything.
     - The link to the portfolio or landing page.
     - One question or next step ("When is a good time for a 10-minute call?" / "What's the deadline?").
     - 4–6 lines in total.
- **Answer what they asked for.** If they asked for rates, availability or specific experience, include it honestly.
- The user sends both. Log one row with `place=<group>` and `notes=supplier request; comment+DM`.

## How to show a pick
```
Group: <group name>
Post in short: <one line>
Score: <score> — <one line why>
Draft:
<the comment>
```

## Example (fictional)
Post: owner of a 6-person accounting office; clients send documents by WhatsApp, email and paper, and every April the team spends days chasing missing files. Asks for a simple tool.

Draft:
> Before buying a tool, check what you already have. Many office systems include a client upload form nobody switched on.
> If not: a fixed document list per client plus one automatic weekly reminder saves most of the chasing.
> Most important: get everything into one channel, not three.

What the owner changed before sending: shorter, more personal, one emoji, merged the last two points. Learn from such edits and offer to add the pattern to the "Voice" section of the profile.

## Placing a draft (only if the user asks)
- Facebook sends a comment on Enter. Type line breaks with Shift+Enter, never press Enter, and never click the send arrow.
- After placing it, tell the user to read it and send it.

## Logging
`channel=facebook`, `place=<group name>`, link to the post, the author's display name (or "anonymous"), a short summary of what they asked, the sent text.
