# Upwork guide

## Scanning
- Run the job searches from `channels.md`, sorted by most recent, with "payment verified" on.
- Only fresh jobs: posted in the last day or two.

## First screen (the default standards; let the user adjust them in the profile)
- Payment verified.
- Client rating 4.5 or higher (or a new client with a clear, serious post).
- Client has spent money before (a meaningful amount).
- Not already crowded: if the client is interviewing many freelancers, or there are 50+ proposals, usually skip.
- Read the client's past reviews and hire history for warning signs (very low rates paid, many open jobs, no feedback).
- The job's requirements fit the user's real experience. Never stretch it.

These are a first screen only. A job that is an especially strong fit can be shown even if it misses one numeric point (age, rating, spend, competition); say which point and why. Never relax a red flag.

## Red flags: always skip
- Many jobs and hires with a tiny total spend (for example 100+ jobs, 90+ hires, under $1,000 spent). That is a free-labor or rating-farming account.
- $5–$10 budgets for real work (a full website, a campaign, an app).
- Jobs across unrelated fields from the same client (a spa website, a music teaser, a book illustration…).
- All-5.0 reviews on tiny jobs.
- Anything that asks to install apps on a phone, use a SIM, or move money.
- Payment method not verified.

## Interest, not only fit
A good client does not make a job worth doing. Prefer substantive work in the user's field over a quick task that teaches nothing and shows nothing. Budget is not a filter unless the user set one.

## How to show a job
```
<title> — <link>
Posted: <when> · Client: <rating> (<reviews>), spent <amount>, <country>
Proposals: <n> · Interviewing: <n> · Connects: <cost>
Why it fits: <one line>
To check: <risks / gaps, honestly>
From past reviews: <one line>
Misses: <filter, if any>
```
Show at most 3. Zero is a valid result: then say so, and name the closest miss and why.

## Lessons from real screening decisions
- A great description does not outweigh three warning signs together (excluded location + new unverified account + rate far below the level).
- A strong client with a long line is still a long line (20–50 proposals, 9 already interviewing).
- A good client does not make a job worth doing (a one-call settings change for an excellent client).
- Showing a strong-fit job with a weak client is right; deciding is the user's. The agent shows, the user decides.

Log every job opened, with `shown` or `skipped` and a short reason, so the user can see later which searches bring jobs worth applying to.

## Drafting a proposal
- Answer exactly what the client asked, including any screening questions and any phrase they ask applicants to start with.
- Open with their problem, not the user's biography.
- One or two relevant work samples, only real ones from the profile.
- A clear, simple plan for the first step and a realistic timeline.
- Personal, natural tone; short.

## Never
- No email, phone, WhatsApp, external links for contact, or off-platform meeting links before a contract.
- No suggestion to pay outside Upwork.
- No invented experience, reviews or results.

## Sending
- The user submits the proposal on Upwork. Never click Submit or Apply, and never spend Connects for the user.

## Logging
`channel=upwork`, `place=<job title>`, job link, client (country, rating, spent), what they need, a short note of the proposal.
