# LinkedIn guide

## The goal on LinkedIn: contracts, not employment

On LinkedIn, First Leads looks for **client work**: projects, retainers, freelance and fractional engagements, consulting. It does not look for jobs where the user would become an employee. Every candidate is judged by one question: could this become a paid engagement where the user is the supplier?

## Scanning
- Run the content searches from `channels.md` (search → Posts, sorted by latest, past 24 hours or past week).
- Also check the user's own feed for posts from their network that describe the pain.
- Optionally check LinkedIn Jobs with the **Contract** (and, if relevant, Part-time) job-type filter and Remote, for project work that fits the offer. Skip full-time employee roles.
- Read the full post and the top comments before scoring.

## Good candidates
- Founders, owners or managers describing the problem the user solves, asking for recommendations, or sharing a struggle.
- "Looking for a freelancer / consultant / agency / someone who can help with…" posts.
- Posts announcing a trigger moment from the profile (new business, launch, funding, expansion) that usually creates outside work.
- Contract, project-based or fractional roles in the user's field.
- Posts with some engagement but room to add a real point.

## Skip
- Full-time or permanent employee jobs, and recruiter posts for them.
- Viral posts with hundreds of comments (the reply gets lost).
- Sales pitches and engagement-bait.

## Supplier requests
When someone posts "I need a…", "looking for a freelancer who…", or asks people to comment or message: show it first with the label `SUPPLIER REQUEST`. Draft a one-line comment that answers their main requirement, and a short message (or a connection request with a one-line note, if not connected) with one or two relevant samples and the portfolio link. If the post says how to apply (comment "interested", send an email, a form), do exactly that instead.

## Drafting comments
- Add one real insight or a concrete experience-based tip related to the post. 2–5 lines.
- No pitch, no link, no "let's connect" in the comment.

## Connection requests
- Only to people the user already exchanged comments with.
- Note: empty, or one personal line referring to the exchange ("Enjoyed our exchange on your post about X").
- After they accept: continue the conversation, ask about their situation. No pitch in the first message.
- Respect the daily limit in the platform rules.

## Never
- No automation tools or extensions that act on the user's behalf.
- No bulk connection requests, no messaging sequences.

## Placing a draft (only if the user asks)
- Enter can send in LinkedIn messages. Use Shift+Enter for line breaks, never press Enter, never click Post or Send.

## Logging
`channel=linkedin`, `place=post|comment|connection|message`, link, what the post was about (no names), the sent text.
