# The First Leads method

## The idea

A new business does not have a budget for campaigns or years to wait for word of mouth. But it has something campaigns cannot buy: the owner's own voice and time. Every day, people ask in public for exactly the help the user offers. The method is to show up in those conversations with real help, consistently, and to learn from what happens.

This method comes from real practice: it was developed by a business owner to get past these exact problems, and it brought paying retainers, from more than one channel, from people with no prior acquaintance: no friends, no referrals, no existing network.

## Spread the risk

Never depend on one channel. A group can change its rules, an account can be restricted, a platform can change its algorithm overnight. Run the method in at least two channels (for example a community channel and LinkedIn or Upwork), so that one channel going quiet never means zero leads. The weekly review checks that the user's leads do not all come from one place.

## The engine

The engine is the same in every channel:

**Find → Score → Draft → Human sends → Log → Learn**

## Find

Look for posts and jobs where a person describes a problem the user solves. They often describe the problem without naming it ("my leads disappear after the demo" is a follow-up problem, even if they never say "follow-up"). Search by situation and pain, not only by keywords. Fresh posts (hours, not weeks) matter most: a late reply rarely starts a conversation.

## Score

Score each candidate 1–5 and show only 4s and 5s:
- **Fit:** is this the user's kind of problem and the user's kind of client (from `profile.md`)?
- **Intent:** is the person asking for help now, or just sharing an opinion?
- **Freshness:** posted recently enough that a reply still matters?
- **Room:** is there still space to add something useful, or have 30 people already said it?
- **Rules:** does the place allow this kind of reply?

It is better to miss a few good posts than to spend the user's time on bad matches.

### Two kinds of posts
- **A help question:** someone describes a problem or asks for advice. Reply in the thread with real help. No private message unless they start one.
- **A supplier request:** someone says they need a provider ("I need a video editor…", "looking for someone who can…"). This is an invitation. Reply briefly in the thread and send a tailored private message with the most relevant proof and the portfolio link, unless the post or the group says comments only. Speed matters most here.

## Draft

A good reply:
- Answers the person's actual question first, with something they can use today.
- Is specific to their situation (their industry, their words), not generic.
- Sounds like the user: use the examples and the "never write" list in `profile.md`.
- Is short: 3–6 lines. No buzzwords, no "As an expert…", no self-praise.
- Leaves a door open, without pushing: an offer of something concrete ("If it helps, I can send you a template for that follow-up message") or a real question about their situation.

A bad reply:
- Talks about the user instead of the person ("I help businesses like yours…").
- Is a pitch, a link dump, or "DM me".
- Could be pasted under any post.
- Repeats what others already wrote in the thread. If someone already gave good advice, build on it ("Adding to what X said…").

### Example

Post: "I have a software product for car garages. Leads see the demo, love it, and then disappear. Ideas?"

Good:
> This often happens when the meeting ends with "let's talk" instead of a set next step. A few things that work:
> End every demo with a date in the calendar for the next step, before you leave the table.
> Ask in the meeting who else has to approve. In some garages it's the importer, so the person who loved it can't move alone.
> Send a short message the same day, on WhatsApp rather than email: two or three things they said they liked, and the next step.
> If you want, I can send you a template for that follow-up message.

Bad:
> Great question! I'm an expert in lead generation and I help businesses close more deals. DM me and let's talk!

## Human sends

The user reads, changes what does not sound like them, and sends. Reasons this step stays human:
1. **Speed converts.** The person who sends can answer the reply right away, in whatever channel it comes (thread, DM, WhatsApp, a call).
2. **Replies come from everywhere.** Only a person sees and handles all of them.
3. **The voice that comments is the voice the lead will talk to.** If the first message is a template, the first real conversation starts with a gap in trust.
4. **Platform rules.** Automated posting gets accounts restricted.

## Log

Every sent item goes into `leads-log.csv` the same day. Without the log there is no learning, only a feeling.

## Learn

Once a week, look at the log: which places bring replies and conversations (not likes), which openings work, which posts were a waste of time. Move time toward what works. Drop places that bring nothing after two weeks.

## What counts as success

- A **reply** is a signal.
- A **conversation** (DM, call, real back-and-forth) is the goal of this stage.
- A **client** is the result, and it usually comes after several conversations.

Track conversations per 10 comments as the main number.
