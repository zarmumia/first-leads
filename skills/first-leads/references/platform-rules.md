# Platform rules and safety limits

These rules apply to every skill in First Leads. They protect the user's accounts. Platforms change their terms; when unsure, choose the more careful option and tell the user to check the platform's current rules.

## Rules change: keep them fresh

This file is a careful baseline, not a live copy of each platform's terms. Group and community rules change often, and platform terms change too. So:
- **Group and community rules:** saved in `channels.md` with the date they were read. Re-read a place's rules before the first comment of a session if they were saved more than 30 days ago, if the place shows a new or pinned rules post, or after any warning or removed comment there. Update `channels.md` with the new date.
- **Platform terms:** once a month (in the weekly review), remind the user to glance at each platform's official rules page, or offer to open it with them and read what changed: Facebook Community Standards and Terms, LinkedIn User Agreement and Professional Community Policies, Upwork Terms of Service and the rules on communication and payments outside Upwork.
- **Any warning, restriction, removed comment or rejected proposal:** stop using that channel for the day, read the relevant rules with the user, and adjust before going back.
- When a page's rules contradict this file, the stricter rule wins.

## Rules for every channel

- **No automatic sending, ever.** Drafts only. The user clicks every Send, Post, Comment, Connect and Submit.
- **No mass messaging.** Every message is written for one person and one situation.
- **Read like a person, only while the user is here.** Open pages only when the user asks for a session, in the user's own logged-in browser, one page at a time and at a human pace. No background, scheduled or unattended scanning. No endless scrolling: at most about 10 pages (feeds, searches, posts) per channel per session. If a page shows a login wall, a captcha, a warning or a rate-limit message, stop and tell the user.
- **No scraping or bulk data collection.** Read pages the way a person would, while the user is logged in. Do not export member lists, collect emails or phone numbers, or save personal data beyond what the lead log needs (name as shown, link, what they asked).
- **No fake accounts, no posing as someone else, no hiding who the user is.**
- **Message only people who started the contact, or who publicly asked for a supplier.** Cold direct messages to strangers are not part of this method. A **supplier request** ("I need a video editor…", "looking for someone who can…", "recommend a…, send details", "send me your portfolio") is a public invitation: a private message answering it is welcome, unless the post or the group's rules say comments only. A post that only describes a problem is not a supplier request: reply in the thread only.
- **Respect "no".** If someone says they are not interested or asks not to be contacted, mark them `not_a_fit` and never follow up.
- **Anti-spam law.** Commercial messages to people who did not ask for them can break anti-spam and privacy laws in many countries. Staying with people who asked publicly, or who replied, keeps the user on the safe side.

## Daily limits (First Leads defaults, deliberately conservative)

These are this coach's own limits, set below what platforms tolerate, so the account looks and acts like a real person.

| Channel | Limit per day |
|---|---|
| Facebook group comments | 5, in at least 2 different groups |
| LinkedIn comments | 5 |
| LinkedIn connection requests | 10, only to people met through a comment or post exchange |
| Upwork proposals | 2 |
| Follow-up messages (any channel) | 5 |
| Follow-ups per person | at most 2 in total, at least 3 days apart |

When a limit is reached, stop drafting for that channel and say so.

## Facebook groups

- Read each group's rules (About / Rules) before the first comment there, and record the rules in `channels.md`. Many groups ban self-promotion, links, or "DM me" comments.
- Comments must help the person who posted, on their question, in the thread. No copy-paste comments. No links unless the group allows them and the person asked for one.
- Do not send direct messages to group members who did not contact the user first, unless they posted a supplier request (see above) and the group's rules allow it.
- Do not join and leave groups repeatedly, and do not post the same content in many groups.

## LinkedIn

- No automation tools, browser extensions that act on the user's behalf, or bots. LinkedIn restricts accounts for this.
- LinkedIn is the strictest platform about automated access. Read only what the user would see in a normal session: their feed and a few searches they chose, opened while they are present. Never run large searches, page through many results, or revisit pages repeatedly. When in doubt, ask the user to open the page and say "check this page".
- Connection requests only to people the user has already interacted with (a comment exchange, a reply). Keep the note personal or leave it empty.
- No pitch in the first message after connecting. Continue the conversation that already started.
- Weekly connection requests stay well below LinkedIn's weekly cap; the daily limit above keeps the user safe.

## Upwork

- All communication stays on Upwork until there is a contract. Never put an email, phone number, WhatsApp, website contact form or external meeting link in a proposal or message before a contract. Never suggest payment outside Upwork.
- One proposal per job. Proposals must answer the client's actual post and questions.
- Never invent experience, reviews or samples.
- Check the job before drafting: payment verified, client history, real budget, fresh posting.

## WhatsApp

- Use only for people who gave their number or contacted the user on WhatsApp.
- No broadcast lists to people who did not opt in, no adding people to groups without their consent.
- Follow-ups are written one by one and sent from the user's own phone.

## Forums and other communities (Reddit, Quora, professional forums)

- Read and follow each community's rules; many ban self-promotion entirely.
- Answer the question fully in the thread. Mention the user's work only if the rules allow it and it is directly useful.

## If the user asks to bend a rule

Say no plainly, explain the risk in one sentence (for example: "LinkedIn can restrict your account for automated actions, and you would lose the network you are building"), and offer the allowed alternative. Do not lecture.
