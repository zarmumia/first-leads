# Goals: adapting First Leads to what the user is looking for

The engine never changes: find people talking about the problem, help them for real, open a conversation, log it, learn. What changes is **what the conversation is for**. The goal is set in the onboarding interview and stored in `profile.md` under "Goal". A user can have more than one goal; ask which one comes first.

| Goal | Typical user | What a good conversation leads to | Proof that matters | Success number |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clients** | Freelancer, service provider, small business | A call about paid work | Past work, before/after, testimonials | Conversations → paid clients |
| **Case-study / pilot partners** | Founder with a product or a new service | A business that agrees to try it, often free or discounted, in exchange for results and a case study | Demo, clear pilot offer (scope, time, what they get, what you ask) | Pilots started → case studies published |
| **Collaborations / partnerships** | Founder or service provider looking for distribution, referrals or complementary partners | A call about working together (referral, bundle, co-marketing, integration) | Who you serve, what you bring to the partner, results so far | Partnership calls → active partnerships |
| **Early users / feedback (discovery)** | Founder before or right after launch | A discovery conversation or a beta signup | The problem stated clearly, a demo or waitlist | Discovery conversations → beta users |

## How each step adapts

**Find (search signals):**
- Clients: people asking for help, recommendations or a provider.
- Pilot partners and early users: people complaining about the problem the product solves, describing workarounds, or asking "is there a tool for…".
- Partnerships: people who serve the same audience with a different offer (they post about their clients' problems, ask for referrals, look for "someone who does X for my clients").

**Score:** "Intent" means intent toward the user's goal. For pilot partners, a strong signal is someone who has the problem now and is actively looking. For partnerships, someone with an audience that overlaps and an offer that does not compete.

**Draft:**
- Always help first, in the thread. Never pitch the product in the comment.
- The open door matches the goal:
  - Clients: a concrete offer of help or a question about their situation.
  - Pilot partners / early users: a question about how they handle it today ("How do you deal with that now?"). The pilot offer comes later, in a private conversation they started or agreed to.
  - Partnerships: point out the overlap honestly, in a reply to their post or in a message only after an exchange ("Sounds like we serve the same owners from different sides").
- Communities are less tolerant of product posts than of advice. Follow each place's rules strictly.

**Get ready (proof):**
- Clients: work, examples, testimonials (at least one, ideally three; volunteer work if needed).
- Pilot partners / early users: a short demo (video or GIF), a one-paragraph pilot offer, and a landing page with a waitlist or a "join the pilot" contact.
- Partnerships: a one-pager on who you serve, what you offer, and what a partner gets.

**Follow-ups:** the next step matches the goal: a call (clients), a pilot kickoff with a clear scope and end date (pilots), an intro call (partnerships), a beta invite or a 20-minute discovery call (early users).

**Weekly review:** track the success number for each goal separately. A founder who gets many discovery calls but no pilots needs a clearer pilot offer, not more comments.

## Lead log
Add the goal to every row in the `notes` column (`goal=clients|pilot|partner|discovery`) so the weekly review can separate them.
